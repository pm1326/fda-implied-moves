## Capricor: deramiocel (cell therapy for heart disease in Duchenne muscular dystrophy)

**Make or break.** Capricor has no approved product and this is its lead programme.

- The FDA **rejected it once already**, in July 2025, citing insufficient evidence of effectiveness and manufacturing issues.
- Review restarted in March 2026 after the phase 3 HOPE-3 trial read out.
- An **FDA advisory panel voted 9 to 3 against** recommending approval on 29 July 2026.
- The decision date was pushed from 22 August to 22 November after the company sent 24-month follow-up data and asked for a **narrower indication**.
- Cash: one report gives about $279m, funding operations into late 2027. That figure predates the panel vote and may be stale.

**If it fails:** a second rejection after a negative panel would leave no clear path for the drug. Expect a very large fall, a likely need to raise money at a low price, and questions over the company's future.

**If it passes:** approval against a negative panel vote would be a surprise, and the share could rise sharply. A recent headline says investors are watching for labelling hints.

**Reading the market:** options imply a move of close to 100%. Given the history above, that is the market correctly pricing a coin flip with extreme outcomes, not a mistake.

## Savara: molgramostim (inhaled treatment for autoimmune pulmonary alveolar proteinosis, a rare lung disease)

**Make or break.** It is Savara's only product candidate.

- Priority review. The decision moved from 22 August to 22 November after the company's answers to FDA questions counted as a major amendment.
- The company says the FDA's letter raised **no safety, effectiveness or manufacturing concerns**.
- Cash: about $130m reported in the third quarter, plus up to about **$150m of financing that only arrives if the drug is approved**.

**If it fails:** the launch financing does not arrive and the company has nothing else to sell. Things to watch even on approval: a demand for more trials or a restrictive label.

**If it passes:** the company moves to a funded launch.

## BridgeBio: BBP-418 (for limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2I/R9)

**Not make or break.** BridgeBio already sells Attruby, which brought in about $222m in one quarter of 2026, and has raised about $1bn to fund three launches.

- Priority review, and the FDA does not plan an advisory panel.
- Small market: about 7,000 patients in the US and Europe.
- Approval may also earn a priority review voucher, which can be sold.

**If it fails:** a setback for one of three launches. Revenue from Attruby is unaffected.

**If it passes:** a second product and a voucher. Helpful, but a small part of the company's value.

## Cogent: bezuclastinib (for gastrointestinal stromal tumours, with sunitinib)

**Major, but not all-or-nothing.** Cogent has no approved product, but the same drug has three separate decisions:

| Use | Decision date |
|---|---|
| Gastrointestinal stromal tumours | 30 November 2026 |
| Non-advanced systemic mastocytosis | 30 December 2026 |
| Advanced systemic mastocytosis | 29 June 2027 |

- Priority review and no advisory panel planned for the first.
- Safety so far is described as in line with similar drugs.

**If it fails:** the first rejection would raise doubts about the other two applications, since it is the same molecule. But the largest market is thought to be mastocytosis, decided a month later.

**If it passes:** a first approval makes the next two more likely.

## What this taught me about the model

- "High dependence" is not one thing. Capricor (one drug, one decision, rejected before) and Cogent (one drug, three decisions) are both "high" in my data but carry very different risk.
- The data cannot see an advisory panel vote or a previous rejection. Those change the approval odds a lot and have to come from research like this.
